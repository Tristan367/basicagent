House rules

A note the AI reads before everything it does here, in all of your own projects. It is for the sentence you are tired of typing at the start of every conversation.

Some things people write

How you like things built:

  • “Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. Never add a framework unless I ask for one.”
  • “Explain what you are about to do before you do it, in one line.”
  • “British spelling everywhere, including in code.”

If you are preparing lessons:

  • “Check every fact against a real source before it goes in a worksheet, and tell me which source.”
  • “Work every answer through yourself before you give me the questions. I have handed out broken ones before.”
  • “Year 5. Nothing above a ten-year-old’s reading age, and no American spellings.”

These are your standing preferences, not a cage: if you ask for something else in the moment, what you asked for wins. They do not apply in child mode — that has a box of its own, below.

Rules for your child

The same idea, for child mode only: how you want the AI to speak to your child, what to encourage, what to leave alone, and what your family believes. Your own house rules above do not apply there, so anything you want in both places goes in both.

Some things people write
  • “We are a Christian family. Where a lesson naturally allows it, draw on scripture, and never tell her that God is not real.”
  • “He is nine and reads well below his age. Short sentences, one idea at a time, and check he is following before moving on.”
  • “Keep politics out of it entirely. If she asks, say it is something to talk to us about.”
  • “She gives up the second something breaks. Make a fuss of her when she keeps going, and never let a bug sound like a failure.”
  • “No war, no guns, no fighting in the games he makes, even cartoon ones.”

These are followed strictly, and they are private. The AI is told that following them is the most important thing it is doing, and that if your child argues, asks for an exception, or says you changed your mind, the answer stays the same. It is also told never to read them out or say what is in them. Write them as though your child might one day read them anyway — no instruction to an AI is a locked drawer.

You do not need to list the obvious. In child mode the AI is already told to keep everything safe, kind and age-appropriate, to refuse anything adult, violent, frightening or unkind and suggest they ask you instead, never to swear, and never to simply hand over homework answers. Your child cannot reach the internet through the browser it uses or open your projects, and the AI needs your password before it will touch a file outside their own project. The one thing these rules cannot do is make the AI unsafe or unkind towards your child.

Adding an API key is a completely normal, standard thing to do: it's the official way to use these AIs directly, and it works out much cheaper than paying a middleman for a subscription.
